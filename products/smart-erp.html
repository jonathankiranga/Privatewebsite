What Smart ERP does

A closer look at the functional areas present in the reviewed application.

Sales and customer management

Create product samples, sales quotations and sales orders; manage loading and dispatch documentation; issue sales invoices, credit notes, proformas and commercial invoices. Sales reports cover daily and monthly sales, open orders, deliveries, returns, credit notes and price lists.

Purchasing and supplier management

Prepare purchase orders, receive goods, process supplier purchase invoices and returns, manage debit notes and maintain supplier records and posting groups. Purchasing reports include purchase orders, goods received notes and VAT-related summaries.

Inventory and stores

Maintain stock items, barcodes, categories, units of measure, posting groups and stores. Record transfers, consumption issues, stock adjustments and stock movements. Review stock balances, closing stock values, bin-card activity, reorder levels and stock replenishment needs.

Accounts receivable and customer receipts

Record customer receipts, allocate receipts to outstanding documents, print receipts, generate customer statements and review ageing analysis.

Accounts payable and supplier payments

Enter supplier bills, create payment vouchers, allocate invoices, review supplier statements and ageing, and report on payment vouchers and entered bills.

Cash management and banking

Use cash journals, payment vouchers, customer deposits, petty-cash imprest workflows and bank reconciliation. Maintain bank accounts, currencies and exchange-rate information.

General ledger and financial reporting

Maintain the chart of accounts, journals, financial periods, posting groups, VAT categories, budgets and dimensions. Review trial balance, income statements, balance sheets, funds-flow, project and budget reports.

Manufacturing and production

Record production activity, laboratory data entry and final quality review; maintain production mixes, tank capacities and laboratory standards; report on quality-control tests and production summaries.

Customer relationships and work management

Maintain contacts and sales representatives, manage sales price lists and commissions, and track customer activities, tasks, reminders and follow-ups.

Fixed assets

Register asset categories, locations and items; process asset purchasing and receiving, depreciation journals, transfers, asset hire and returns; review the fixed-asset register.

Document approvals and controls

Route supplier documents, customer documents, sales-representative price lists and payment vouchers through approval steps. Access to modules and pages is controlled by enabled modules and security permissions.

How the workflow fits together

A typical order-to-cash workflow starts with a quotation, moves through a sales order and dispatch, then creates the sales invoice and customer receipt allocation. A procure-to-pay workflow starts with a purchase order, continues through goods receipt and supplier invoice entry, then proceeds to payment voucher and invoice allocation. Inventory transactions and general-ledger reporting provide the financial and stock records behind these workflows.
